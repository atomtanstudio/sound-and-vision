# H3 kinetic type

Distilled on 2026-10-04 from MiniMax Design's own skills, rewritten in English and adapted (not translated):
`music-video-subtitle-generator` 0.6.13, `h3-visual-design` 1.0.3 (its typography-packaging route and references),
`cool-music-video` 1.0.3, `h3-prompt-expert` 1.0.4, `anime-pv-maker` 0.1.3, `typography-product-tvc` 1.0.3 and
`mg-voiceover-animation-generator` 0.1.7 (versions from their `meta.yaml` in `~/.hub-global/skills/`), plus MiniMax
Design.app's `mv` workflow (typography packaging, video generation, storyboard, post), its MiniMax H3 vendor card and its
on-image-text note. The machine facts were read from ComfyUI's node source and the `lab/living-album/` runs. "MiniMax does X"
reports their rule; "we" marks our adaptation; anything not run by us is marked untested.

## How it fits with motion-studio

- **motion-studio** (`.claude/skills/motion-studio/SKILL.md`) governs graphics drawn in code: the render contract (a pure
  function of `t`, seeded noise), its style guide, storyboard and audio-cue files, and the review gate.
- **This skill** governs typography that H3 generates: pixels from a model, different on every seed, spelling not guaranteed.
- **A hybrid uses both**: code draws the exact lettering (a still, a short hold, or an overlay) under motion-studio's rules,
  H3 builds the world and the motion around it, and a code overlay can replace any word H3 gets wrong. An H3 clip is a
  fixed asset the film places by song time, so the assembled film still meets the render contract.
- Plan the same way: the song's style guide and a storyboard on the beat grid first (motion-studio's files), then one H3
  prompt per storyboard row. The finished film passes motion-studio's review gate plus the checks in section 5.

## 1. What H3 does with words

**What we know**

- H3 can paint words into the video: MiniMax's MV pipeline generates its big kinetic type inside the model by default. It
  does not guarantee spelling: MiniMax registers every lyric or title that must be exact for a deterministic overlay in post,
  and their knowledge base puts critical video text in a post overlay. Text baked into a generated reference video we
  studied wobbled and garbled ("REITLLS BESX BUT").
- **Few and short.** Latin hero: 1–3 short words (MiniMax's heavy and tech styles set them in capitals). CJK hero:
  1–4 characters (a poster headline 2–8). One main word or short phrase per shot; at most one main title plus one group of
  supporting words in a frame; 2–4 new readable words per 12–15 s. A long exact line is split into 2–3 meaning-lines that assemble on
  one grid, never squeezed into one strip, and a whole lyric is never set as subtitles.
- **Size.** Hero 45–120% of frame width, mid 18–55%, small 6–18%. Our clips are about 1024 px wide; we treat H3-drawn letters
  under ~5% of frame height (~50 px) as unreliable and draw small text in code (our rule of thumb, not yet measured).
- **Letterforms, not font names.** H3 doesn't know "Helvetica". Write skeleton, width, weight (550–950), terminals,
  counters, case, tracking, material, color, size and place (`references/type-system.md`).
- **Latin vs CJK.** CJK keeps radicals whole, crossings clear and its square center; horizontal compression ≤12–15%; reveal
  whole characters, never stroke flicker; particles may gather along strokes but never shatter a character. Mixed scripts
  get separate roles and baselines (main 100%, second 22–45%) and never translate each other line for line; a Chinese word
  must not have an English synonym in the same event, or the model swaps it.
- **Readable first.** Every word is complete and still for 0.55–1.0 s (longer for more words) before anything deforms,
  slices, glitches, is occluded or leaves; a glitch lasts 2–4 frames and comes back exactly spelled.
- **Drift is per frame.** A word right in its hold can be wrong mid-move, so check every frame that shows text.
- **H3 adds cuts by itself.** Given a still and only "locked-off camera" it cut to close-ups and back in three of four clips
  (`server/sv/albums.py`); "one continuous shot, no cuts" stopped it. Say whether a shot may cut inside itself.

**MiniMax's rules for exact text (all of them apply)**

1. **Whitelist.** List every exact on-screen string once, in quotes, with the same case and punctuation every time it
   appears, and add: "Only these strings may ever be readable; no other letters, numbers, signs, logos, captions or
   subtitles at any time." A word that builds word by word may show its leading part while building; say so.
2. **Exact copy.** The words come from the master take's locked lyric, with `song.timing` corrections applied. Never
   rewrite, translate, paraphrase, add or "fix" them. The one change we allow is the case the song's look sets (`caps`),
   applied to every word. If a word is uncertain, ask; never take words from listening.
3. **One main typography event per shot**, one strongest motion at a time; the other layers settle or hold.
4. **Never over eyes or the main expression**; keep the mouth clear whenever someone sings.
5. **Lyric windows.** A word appears no earlier than it's sung and is gone, or broken into unreadable fragments, after its
   window. Stretches with no lyric say "no readable text, only non-text marks".
6. **Instruction words stay off screen.** Role names, style names, color names and hex values never go inside quotes. No
   pseudo-UI, codes, coordinates, issue numbers, fake data or filler English ("FUTURE", "FLOW", "GRID").
7. **Picture first when there's a lot of text** (more than ~3 exact strings, a poster or title card): make a still with the
   exact lettering and give it to H3 as `<Picture 1>`, saying it is a picture to keep, not text to redraw (route b).

## 2. One lyric shot (5–15 s)

MiniMax's typography prompt has nine blocks (setup, references, core idea, subject and space, type/color/layout system,
music, copy and whitelist, timed composition states, continuity), aims at 5,600–6,200 characters and never exceeds 7,000
(their API's input cap, counted character by character with spaces and newlines). Most of it, 2,600–3,400, goes to the timed
states. The floor exists to catch prompts that were summarized: what a vague prompt leaves out, H3 fills with its defaults
(generic 3D type, swapped fonts, translated or invented words, MiniMax notes). When over budget they cut repeated adjectives first, then merge repeated identity and limit lines, then merge
synonymous sentences; exact text, times, type roles and spatial relations are never cut.

We keep H3's official three fields (`integrated_multimodal_description`, `overall_soundscape`, `non_diegetic_music`) and
put those blocks inside the first, in English. Budget: 2,500–4,500 characters for 5–8 s, 4,000–6,500 for 9–15 s, never
over 7,000 (count with `wc -m`). States: 1–2 for 4–5 s, 2–3 for 6–8 s, 3–4 for 9–12 s, 3–5 for 13–15 s; each state has
one cue and one strongest move.

Timeline grammar, written into every state:

- a word: `cue → enters → complete and still → one response → depth or layout change → settles or exits`
- a state: `cue → carrier enters → type locks to the grid → hero readable → one focal response → an element becomes the next state`
- an effect: `source → path → contact → destination` (nothing from empty air)
- a word in space: `discover → attach → track → occlude → transfer → settle`

**The template** (replace every `<…>` except the `<Picture N>` tags, which H3 reads; drop lines that don't apply)

```text
[first frame only] For the target video, at 0.00 seconds into the target video, <Picture 1> (from [Shot 1]) is fully referenced.
[first + last frame only] How the reference pictures align with the target video — <Picture 1> (from [Shot 1]) aligns with the 0.00-second mark of the target video; <Picture 2> (from [Shot 1]) aligns with the <8.00>-second mark of the target video.

integrated_multimodal_description: [Shot 1] STYLE LOCK: <the song's header, word for word the same in every shot: medium, palette with a job and hex for each color, grain/texture, light direction, cut language>.
SHOT: <8.0> seconds, <16:9>, <one continuous shot with no cuts | internal hard cuts at …>. A kinetic lyric shot in the <chorus> of a <96> BPM song; the words land with the sung vocal.
IDEA: <one sentence: the world, the one typography event, and what hands off to the next shot>.
WORLD: <floor; back plane; front/middle/back depth; vanishing direction; key and rim light; a moving highlight and which type layer answers it>.
CAMERA: <start → path and speed → end; what it never does>.
TYPE CAST: Display A: <skeleton, width, weight, terminals, counters, case, tracking, material, color, size>. Display B: <contrasts with A on two axes>. Utility: <or none>. Marks: <non-text only>.
COLOR SCRIPT: <state 1 → which colors swap → the resting palette>.
MECHANISMS: <1–2 from type-system, described by what is seen>.
ON-SCREEN TEXT, exact and case-sensitive: "<WORDS>" (Display A, <how it builds>) and "<second line>" (Display B). Only these strings may ever be readable, each only in its window below. No other letters, numbers, signs, logos, captions or subtitles at any time.
STATE 1 | 0.00-<t> s | <frame and camera>. <cue> brings <carrier> in from <edge>. <No readable text | "<WORD>" enters how, complete from <t>>.
STATE 2 | <t>-<t> s | <as sung at <t>, "<WORD>" …; complete and still from <t> to <t>; one response on the <downbeat> at <t>; who passes in front, when>.
TRANSITION OUT | <t>-<end> s | <how each word leaves or breaks up before its window ends; the stable last 0.3–0.8 s before the cut; which element points into the next shot>.
CONTINUITY: <identity, space, light, palette and letterforms stay the same; one or two risk-specific limits>.

overall_soundscape: <song locked: "The supplied song plays unchanged as the only sound: <tempo, drums, instruments, vocal>; the vocal phrase runs from <t> to <t> seconds. No added sound effects, no other voices." | not locked: "Quiet room tone only.">

non_diegetic_music: <song locked: "The supplied song only; no other music." | not locked: "N/A. Do not add background music.">
```

All times are clip seconds (song time minus the clip's start). A full example with numbers: `references/worked-example.md`.

## 3. A whole song

1. **Read the timing.** Master take: `p.song.master`. Use its `analysis` with the hand corrections applied:
   `takeAnalysis(p, take)` in `web/src/lib/timing.ts` (`song.timing[take][line]` wins over the aligner). It gives `tempo`,
   `beats` (seconds), `downbeat` (the index in `beats` of the first downbeat; bars every four beats if the song is in 4/4,
   which the analysis doesn't record, so confirm the meter by ear), `sections`, and `lines` with `words` (`text`, `start`,
   `end`). On-screen words come from these lines and nowhere else.
2. **Cut points.** Cut on a downbeat (or a snare, drop or hard accent) that falls in a gap between sung words, ideally the
   breath between lines; never inside a word or a held vowel. No downbeat in the gap: the nearest beat inside it.
3. **Shot lengths.** MiniMax splits 30 s into 4–8 shots of 2–5 s for energetic parts; calm verses can hold 8–15 s. H3 was
   trained on ~5.2–15.1 s, so a 2–4 s shot is generated at 124 frames or more and trimmed, or several short shots are written
   as internal hard cuts of one 8–15 s clip (MiniMax's "group": one generation of 4–15 s with its own shots inside; for a
   song of T seconds they make ⌈T/15⌉ groups of nearly equal length, then nudge borders to line ends and downbeats).
4. **Lengths on the grid.** Each clip starts at its shot's song start; its length is the smallest 17k + 5 frames covering
   the shot (table in `references/legion.md`); the tail is trimmed at the cut. Frame `f` is song time `start + f/24`.
5. **Words per shot.** Pick the main word or phrase from the lines inside the shot (title, hook, strong noun, first words of
   a section, the line on the energy peak). It is on screen from its sung start to at most its line's end plus a short hold,
   and gone before the next line. Repeated hooks keep the same Display A and escalate by space, scale, material or exit.
6. **One header.** Write the song's STYLE LOCK paragraph once (from the style guide, the story's style, the cover and
   `references/styles.md`) and paste it word for word into every shot; the type cast never changes between shots.
7. **Joins.** Hard cuts only, unless the style guide says otherwise. When the next shot continues the same space, its
   `first_frame` is this clip's frame at the cut (after trimming). Otherwise keep the header, match the direction of motion
   or cut on a matching shape. Hand the type's momentum across: the outgoing word sweeps out or shatters on the hit, the
   incoming one lands on the next accent. Leave the last 0.3–0.8 s of each shot stable.
8. **Assembly.** Place each clip at its song start and trim at its cut. The master take's audio runs once, continuous, from
   0; clip audio is always dropped (even locked audio). One grain/grade pass over the whole film hides batch differences.

## 4. On the machine

**Measured (2026-10-04, `lab/kinetic-h3/`, four clips of a chorus line with the song locked in):** H3 spelled every word
right, but with text to video it opens with about 1.5 s of scene before acting on the first timed state, so words came
1–2 s after they were sung; a code-drawn frame pinned at each word brought them within about 0.45 s, still not
frame-exact. So for lyric videos the user chose the hybrid: H3 makes a plate with no words at all, and every word is
drawn in code on its sung frame (`lab/kinetic-h3/hybrid.py`, `kt5`; frame-exact). H3-drawn words are for moments whose
timing doesn't matter (a title card, an instrumental hook), never for sung lines.

Three routes, mixed freely per shot (wiring, numbers and what is untested: `references/legion.md`):

| Route | Use it when | Nodes | Exact words |
|---|---|---|---|
| (a) Text to video | 1–3 short words, the style is the point | `MiniMaxH3ImageToVideo` with no pictures | Least sure; keep a code overlay ready |
| (b) Code-drawn still or pinned hold | The lettering must be exact, or there's a lot of it | `first_frame` / `last_frame` of `MiniMaxH3ImageToVideo`; `MiniMaxH3AddGuide` pins a picture or a 5/22/39… frame clip at any frame | Exact where pinned; check between pins |
| (c) The real song inside | Beats and words should land on the actual audio; anyone singing | `MiniMaxH3NativeAudioLock` on the AV latent (or `PixaromaH3AudioSync`, `VRGDG_MiniMaxH3AudioDrive`); `MiniMaxH3AddGuide` with `audio` | Not about spelling |

- Our graph (`lab/living-album/la/h3.py`, `server/sv/video/h3.py`): fused turbo UNET, Qwen3-VL 32B encoder, video VAE,
  6 steps, res_multistep, shift 12/3, 24 fps. Audio VAE for route (c): `minimax_h3_audio_vae_fp32.safetensors`.
- `first_frame` is stretched to the canvas without keeping its aspect: draw it at exactly the generation size. The pictures
  also go to the text encoder, which sees the lettering.
- A pin fixes the whole frame, not only the letters: draw the pinned frame entirely in code (type on a cover or color field),
  or repair a frame from an earlier H3 pass.
- Length 17k + 5 frames, trained ~124–362. Canvas ≤ 768×1344 in area; with the fused UNET use 1024×1024, 1024×576 or
  576×1024. Render time measured: 1024², 12.2 s in ~106 s (models loaded, no audio).
- Submit only when the user asked for a render; check `GET /queue` first; never change ComfyUI's settings, models or nodes.
- Nothing in the app sends lyric shots to H3 yet; this skill is for planning, prompting and lab runs until it's wired.

## 5. Checks

**Before sending a prompt**

- Every quoted string is a whitelisted string (or a leading part of one while it builds), identical to the locked lyric
  after the look's case: letters, apostrophes, punctuation. The "only these strings" sentence is there.
- Every word's window sits inside its sung window from the corrected analysis; nothing appears early; nothing readable is
  left after its window or in a lyric-free stretch.
- One main typography event; each word has a complete, still hold of ≥0.55 s (≥1 s for four or more words) before any
  deformation; nothing over eyes or a singing mouth; no H3-drawn word below ~5% of frame height.
- The STYLE LOCK paragraph is byte-identical to the song's header (diff it); the type cast is unchanged.
- Times fit inside the clip; the length is on the grid; the cut is a downbeat in a gap; cuts inside the shot are allowed or
  forbidden in words.
- Mode line present for first frame or first + last frame; the still is exactly the canvas size.
- Under 7,000 characters, and inside the budget for its length.

**After a clip comes back**

- **Spelling, every frame.** Extract every frame in each word's window (`ffmpeg -ss <a> -to <b> -i clip.mp4 f_%04d.png`) and
  read every word letter by letter. A wrong or missing letter while a word is meant to be readable fails the shot. Outside
  the windows, look for stray readable letters, numbers or signs anywhere; any is a fail.
- **Phone size.** Build motion-studio's contact sheet and its 360 px wide version; the hero must read in its hold.
- **Beat alignment.** Lay the clip on the song at its start: each word first appears within ±2 frames of its sung start
  (our tolerance), never visibly late; responses on their beats; the cut on its frame.
- **Continuity.** Header look holds (palette, grain, light, letterforms); the end frame works as the next shot's start.
- **Then decide:** keep; one new seed when the miss looks like chance (a few bad frames in an otherwise right clip); a
  changed prompt when it doesn't (change something real: MiniMax warns against resending a failed prompt); or keep the clip
  and put the exact word on top in code for its hold (motion-studio governs that overlay). Record what failed and at what time, and say so plainly; a retry that didn't fix it is reported as such.
- The assembled film then goes through motion-studio's three review rounds.
